MODULE 04 · REASONABLE SUSPICION STANDARD
Scenario

The Overheard Conversation

ROOM: 204 PERIOD: 3 ROLE: HOMEROOM TEACHER

You're circulating during independent work time. Two students near the back are talking quietly — not disruptive, just quieter than usual. As you pass, you catch a fragment: one student says something like "…he does that every time, it's not a big deal," and the other goes quiet when she notices you.

You don't have the full conversation. You don't know if "he" is a parent, a sibling, another student, or someone else. The tone wasn't panicked — more resigned than upset.

What do you do in the next 60 seconds?

Standard — reasonable suspicion is a pattern worth a private follow-up, not a conclusion you have to reach first.
The Private Check-In

The check-in

Later in the period, you find a quiet moment and check in with her one-on-one. She says: "It's fine, I don't want to talk about it."

Where do you take it from here?

Standard — a reluctant student doesn't cancel a reporting obligation that already exists.
Closing The Loop

Closing the loop

You now have what you need — what you overheard, and what the student told you in the check-in. The next step is how you record it and route it.

How do you close the loop?

Standard — prompt, factual, documented. Investigation and confirmation are not the teacher's role.
Complete
3/3 — Case closed
You applied the reasonable suspicion standard cleanly at every step.

The takeaway. Reasonable suspicion ≠ certainty. The standard is met by what you observed and were told — not by a full picture.

The teacher's job is notice → private check-in → prompt, factual documentation → handoff to the right channel. Not investigation. Not confirmation. Not silence.
